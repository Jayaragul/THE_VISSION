Krafton pledges $250 million deep tech investment in India to expand beyond gaming

The PUBG publisher plans to inject a massive capital round over the next four years, specifically targeting artificial intelligence and physical robotics.

Saturday, 5 September 2026 2 min read 1 source Credible reporting, no primary

The short version
  • South Korean gaming giant Krafton has announced a fresh $250 million investment commitment in the Indian market over the next four years.
  • The new capital marks a strategic pivot, specifically targeting deep tech, artificial intelligence, and physical robotics startups.
  • This commitment brings Krafton’s total planned investment in India to over $500 million since its market entry in 2021.

South Korean gaming powerhouse Krafton, the publisher of blockbuster titles PUBG and Battlegrounds Mobile India (BGMI), has pledged a fresh $250 million investment in India over the next three to four years, according to reporting from TechCrunch. The massive capital injection represents a major strategic shift for the publisher, which is looking to transition from a pure-play gaming firm into a diversified technology holding company with a primary focus on deep tech.

Unlike Krafton's previous venture capital rounds in India, which focused on game development studios and digital entertainment platforms, this new $250 million fund is specifically mandated to target artificial intelligence, robotics, and deep technology startups. The investment commitment follows a high-profile diplomatic meeting in New Delhi between Krafton’s Chairman Chang Byung-gyu and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, where the two discussed technological collaboration and local talent development.

The commitment cements Krafton’s position as one of the most aggressive foreign corporate venture capital investors in India, bringing its total planned capital deployment in the country to over $500 million since 2021. Indian tech analysts noted that Krafton is leveraging its immense, highly profitable gaming revenue in the country to secure early equity in India's emerging AI and hardware startups, positioning itself as a central player in the nation's broader deep tech ecosystem.

Why it matters

Krafton's strategic pivot highlights how international gaming companies are leveraging their cash-cow properties to finance expensive AI and robotics research in emerging markets. By injecting $250 million into Indian deep tech, Krafton is addressing India's capital gap for early-stage AI startups while aligning with the Indian government's push for local high-tech manufacturing and digital sovereignty. This move could establish India as a major hub for cost-effective AI engineering and physical robotics development.
