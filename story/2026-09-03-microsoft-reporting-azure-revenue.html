Microsoft restructures financial segments, disclosing Azure cloud revenues for first time

The consolidation into two reporting segments — Agents and Infra, and Devices and Consumer — aims to isolate pure cloud consumption figures and reflect the structural impact of AI.

The short version
  • Microsoft is reorganizing its financial reporting from three segments into two: 'Agents and Infra' and 'Devices and Consumer,' according to a report by The Verge.
  • For the first time, Microsoft will disclose exact quarterly revenue figures for its Azure cloud business, which were previously reported only as year-over-year growth percentages.
  • The Azure revenue definition is being adjusted to focus on pure cloud consumption, excluding GitHub, developer cloud services, and Security Copilot, starting with fiscal Q1 2027 in late October.

Microsoft is implementing a major restructuring of its financial reporting segments that will see the tech giant disclose the exact quarterly revenue of its Azure cloud computing business for the first time, The Verge reported on September 3. The reorganization consolidates Microsoft's three traditional business segments into two, reflecting the massive, structural impact that artificial intelligence is having on its operational architecture.

Under the new framework, Microsoft's business is divided into 'Agents and Infra' and 'Devices and Consumer.' The Agents and Infra segment will house Azure, Microsoft 365 Cloud (both commercial and consumer), server products, industry solutions, and its frontier support services. The Devices and Consumer segment will encompass Windows OEM licensing, Xbox, consumer devices, and search and advertising businesses like LinkedIn.

To provide investors with a cleaner view of its cloud growth, Microsoft is adjusting the scope of what is counted as Azure revenue. The new definition will focus purely on cloud consumption, stripping out revenue from GitHub cloud, developer cloud services, Security Copilot, and Healthcare and Life Sciences cloud. These changes will take effect starting with Microsoft's first-quarter fiscal 2027 earnings report, scheduled for release in late October 2026.

Why it matters

Disclosing exact quarterly revenue for Azure is a milestone in transparency that highlights the massive scale of Microsoft's cloud operation. By isolating pure cloud consumption and grouping it with Azure under 'Agents and Infra,' Microsoft is aligning its financial reporting with its strategic bet on AI infrastructure. This segment restructuring will allow investors to directly evaluate the revenue-generating power of its massive AI capital expenditures, rather than relying on muddy year-over-year growth percentages.
