Coding agents

Cognition is in talks to raise at $40bn, three months after a $26bn round

Bloomberg reports the coding company is fielding early investor interest at a valuation more than half again its May mark, on annualised revenue said to be approaching $1bn.

The short version
  • The talks would value Cognition at $40bn or more, against $26bn when it raised $1bn in May 2026.
  • Bloomberg's sources put annualised revenue near $1bn, roughly double the $492m run rate reported at the previous round.
  • The round is early and unconfirmed: Cognition may not raise at all, or may seek different terms.

Cognition, the company behind the Devin coding agent, is in early talks with investors at a valuation of at least $40bn, Bloomberg reported on 12 August. The figure would be more than half again the $26bn valuation the company carried in May 2026, when it raised $1bn.

The justification investors appear to be working from is revenue growth. TechCrunch, following Bloomberg's report, put Cognition's annualised revenue run rate at roughly $1bn, against $492m at the time of the May financing. If accurate, that is a doubling in about three months, and it is the number doing all the work in the valuation.

Several caveats sit on top of this. The reporting rests on unnamed sources, the company has confirmed nothing, and Bloomberg notes Cognition may decide not to raise or may seek financing on different terms. Annualised run rate is also the most flattering revenue measure available to a fast-growing company, since it extrapolates a recent month across a full year.

The wider pattern is firmer than any individual figure. Coding is the one agentic application with clear and measurable willingness to pay, and valuations in the segment have been repricing upward every few months rather than every few years.

Why it matters

A valuation rising by more than half in three months, on revenue the company has not confirmed, is a fair proxy for how private AI markets are currently pricing risk. It is also a bet that coding agents keep their margin as the models underneath them commoditise — which is the question the entire application layer turns on.
