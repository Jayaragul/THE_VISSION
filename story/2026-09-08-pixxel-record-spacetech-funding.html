Pixxel raises record $100 million Series C to expand hyperspectral satellite constellation

The round, co-led by Temasek and Seraphim Space, is the largest single investment in India's space-technology history.

THE VISSION Desk
Tuesday, 8 September 2026 2 min read 1 source Credible reporting, no primary

The short version
  • Indian space-tech company Pixxel secured $100 million in a Series C funding round co-led by Temasek and Seraphim Space.
  • The capital will expand Pixxel's high-resolution Honeybee hyperspectral satellite constellation and develop its Aurora AI software platform.
  • Pixxel has deployed six Firefly satellites to date and holds active imagery contracts with NASA and the U.S. National Reconnaissance Office.

Indian Earth-observation startup Pixxel has closed a record-breaking $100 million Series C funding round, representing the largest single capital injection in the history of India's private space-technology sector. The round was co-led by Singapore state investor Temasek and the UK's Seraphim Space Investment Trust. South Korea's IMM Investment and India’s 360 ONE Asset also joined the round, bringing Pixxel’s total cumulative funding to $195 million since its founding in 2019.

The Bengaluru-based company plans to utilize the funds to accelerate the manufacturing of its next-generation hyperspectral satellite constellation, codenamed Honeybee. Scheduled for its first launches in 2027, the Honeybee constellation will capture high-resolution imagery across hundreds of light wavelengths, including shortwave infrared. This enables the detection of chemical signatures such as crop health anomalies, methane leaks, and mineral reserves that are entirely invisible to conventional optical sensors.

A significant portion of the capital is earmarked for the expansion of Aurora, Pixxel's proprietary, AI-driven Earth-intelligence software platform. Aurora integrates complex multispectral datasets to generate automated, field-level analytics for commercial and governmental clients. The company is also scaling its Indian manufacturing footprint through its advanced 'Gigapixxel' facility while simultaneously expanding its operations in the United States to serve international public-sector customers.

Led by co-founders Awais Ahmed and Kshitij Khandelwal, Pixxel has emerged as a key supplier for national security and scientific agencies, securing imagery contracts with NASA, the U.S. National Reconnaissance Office, and the Indian Ministry of Defence. CEO Awais Ahmed remarked that the fresh capital will allow Pixxel to transition from a niche satellite imagery provider into a global, planetary-scale data infrastructure corporation, servicing civil and commercial sustainability needs worldwide.

Why it matters

This record funding highlights the commercial maturation of hyperspectral Earth observation and its intersection with AI analysis. By combining advanced orbital sensors with automated machine-learning models, Pixxel is building the infrastructure necessary to monitor global climate, agricultural, and industrial changes in real time. For enterprise builders, this expands the availability of high-resolution, chemistry-level data feeds to train predictive environmental models at scale.
