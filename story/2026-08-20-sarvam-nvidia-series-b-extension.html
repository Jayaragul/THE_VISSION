Sovereign AI

Nvidia leads a $74m extension into Sarvam, India's sovereign-model startup

The Bengaluru company reached unicorn status in June at a $1.5bn valuation, and the chipmaker backing it also supplies the GPUs India's sovereign AI programme runs on.

Sarvam AI is close to raising roughly $74 million — about ₹700 crore — in an extension of its Series B round, with Nvidia leading and Glade Brook Capital, Gaja Capital and Indigo Ventures among those participating, according to reporting from Entrackr and Inc42. Nvidia is reported to be contributing ₹238 crore, around $25 million, with Glade Brook at ₹190.3 crore.

The extension follows a first close in June, when Sarvam raised $234 million of a $300 million Series B at a $1.5 billion post-money valuation. That round made it one of only two Indian AI unicorns, alongside Bhavish Aggarwal's Krutrim. Founded in Bengaluru in 2023, Sarvam builds models, infrastructure and applications aimed at enterprises, developers and government agencies, and in February open-sourced two foundation models: a 30-billion-parameter dense model and a 105-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model.

Nvidia leading the round is the detail worth pausing on. India's sovereign AI push — the IndiaAI Mission's compute pool and Krutrim's cloud among it — currently runs on imported accelerators, overwhelmingly Nvidia's. A supplier taking an equity position in the country's most prominent sovereign-model builder deepens a commercial relationship that the word "sovereign" is meant to describe an escape from.

Why it matters

For an Indian founder, this is the clearest signal yet that sovereign-AI capital is available at scale and that the bar is a shipped, open-weight model rather than a policy pitch — Sarvam open-sourced two before the money arrived. The tension worth watching is structural: a national AI programme whose flagship startup is part-owned by the company that also sells it every GPU it trains on has substituted one dependency for a closer one, and that arrangement will shape what "sovereign" can actually mean in Indian procurement.
