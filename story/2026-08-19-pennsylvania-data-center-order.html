Pennsylvania requires local approval before the state will permit new AI data centers

Executive Order 2026-05 also bars nondisclosure agreements on data center deals and strips fast-track permitting from a governor who once championed the buildout.

The short version
  • Gov. Josh Shapiro signed Executive Order 2026-05 on August 18, requiring AI data center developers to sign a binding consent order committing to his GRID standards before Pennsylvania will issue a permit.
  • Projects now need local community approval before the state will approve them, and developers must finance their own power costs rather than passing them to residents.
  • The order bars nondisclosure agreements on data center projects, removes them from the state's Permit Fast Track Program, and requires operators to report energy and water use.

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro signed Executive Order 2026-05 on August 18, requiring companies proposing AI data centers in the state to sign a legally binding consent order committing to his Governor's Responsible Infrastructure Development (GRID) standards before the state's Department of Environmental Protection will process their permit. The GRID standards, first announced in February as a voluntary step, cover energy affordability, environmental protection, local workforce development and transparency.

The order's central mechanism is a local veto: if a host community does not approve a project, the state will not approve it either. "If you can't agree to our strict requirements and get the community where you want to build to say yes," Shapiro said at the signing, "you're not going to have the Commonwealth's support either." Developers must also finance their own power costs rather than passing them on to residents or businesses, with an increasing share required to come from clean sources such as solar, advanced nuclear and battery storage.

The order also strips AI data centers from Pennsylvania's Permit Fast Track Program, bars the use of nondisclosure agreements on data center deals, and requires operators to report their energy and water consumption. Companies that don't comply risk permit denial and the loss of sales-tax exemptions on equipment. More than 100 speculative data center proposals are pending across the state, though only about five have received permits so far, according to Shapiro's office.

The order marks a reversal for a governor who had actively courted the data center buildout, including a $20 billion commitment from Amazon to build at least two facilities in Bucks and Luzerne counties. It follows Pennsylvania's Republican-controlled Senate declining to act on GRID legislation that had already passed the state House, leaving executive action as Shapiro's only near-term option to impose the standards.

Why it matters

For any company planning a data center in Pennsylvania — one of the East Coast's most sought-after sites, given its Marcellus Shale gas supply and existing grid capacity — this converts what had been a voluntary pledge into a hard gate: no local sign-off means no state permit, regardless of the capital committed. Developers elsewhere should expect this playbook to travel, since Pennsylvania is not the only state fielding local opposition to AI infrastructure, and asking the state first has now visibly stopped being the fast path.
