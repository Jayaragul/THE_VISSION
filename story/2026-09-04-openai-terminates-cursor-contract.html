OpenAI terminates Cursor model contract following SpaceX acquisition

Citing trust concerns with Elon Musk's corporate history, OpenAI winds down its model provision agreement with the coding startup.

THE VISSION Desk
Friday, 4 September 2026 2 min read 2 sources Primary source confirmed

The short version
  • OpenAI has announced it is winding down its contract to provide frontier AI models to Cursor, a popular collaborative code editor.
  • The termination is scheduled to take effect on November 12, 2026, following the maximum contractually permitted notice period.
  • OpenAI explicitly cited trust issues with SpaceX owner Elon Musk and his history of contract violations as the reason for the separation.

OpenAI has announced the termination of its contract to provide frontier AI models to the popular collaborative code editor startup, Cursor, following Cursor's acquisition by Elon Musk's SpaceX. According to official blog posts and reporting from Wired, the model provision contract will officially wind down and shut off on November 12, 2026, giving Cursor the maximum notice period permitted under their agreement.

The decision ends a highly lucrative partnership that had lasted nearly four years. Wired reported that OpenAI had recently estimated the Cursor partnership would generate more than $1 billion in annual recurring revenue for the company. Despite the massive financial blow of walking away from this revenue, OpenAI leadership determined that the acquisition by SpaceX created an unacceptable corporate risk.

In an official statement, OpenAI explicitly stated that it cannot be confident SpaceX will use its technology within its terms of service. The company cited Elon Musk's history of corporate contract disputes, including lawsuits and instances of admitting to terms of service violations under oath with his other companies, such as Twitter (now X) and xAI, as the primary basis for the lack of trust.

Why it matters

OpenAI's decision to walk away from a potential $1 billion in annual revenue highlights the extreme corporate rivalry in the AI space. Rather than allowing its frontier models to power a tool owned by Elon Musk's SpaceX, OpenAI chose to terminate the contract, prioritizing protective alignment and competitive boundaries over short-term revenue. This move will force Cursor to rely more heavily on Anthropic's Claude or xAI's proprietary models, further reshaping the landscape of developer-focused AI coding tools.

What this desk does not yet know

Will Cursor be able to migrate its massive developer base to alternative frontier models like Claude or Grok without losing coding accuracy or speed?
