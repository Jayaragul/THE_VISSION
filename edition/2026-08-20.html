An AI designs proteins that beat the competition winners, and a robot maker's debut goes vertical

Anthropic published wet-lab results in which Claude ran a protein design pipeline end to end and hit 14 of 15 targets, beating the winning entry on one of them — with the company itself noting a binder is only the first step toward a drug. Unitree's Shanghai listing rose as much as 629% before closing up 460%, the first mainland humanoid robot maker to go public. India's Sarvam is raising $74m in a round Nvidia is leading, and the chip supply chain tightened at both ends: Samsung raised foundry prices and China slowed critical optical materials to Taiwan.

Protein design

An AI agent ran a protein design pipeline unaided and hit 14 of 15 wet-lab targets

Anthropic reports hit rates of 22.6% to 35.1% against an industry norm of 10-15%, and says the work stays restricted in its public models pending a trusted-access programme.

  • Anthropic reported that Claude models designed protein binders that bound successfully against 14 of 15 targets tested in a wet lab, producing 354 confirmed binders from 1,320 designs.
  • Running against all targets at once in a 48-hour session, Mythos Preview hit 26.7% and Opus 4.8 hit 22.6%; given 24 hours per individual target, Mythos Preview reached 35.1%, against the 10-15% Anthropic calls typical.
  • Against one target, RBX1, the model reached a 40% hit rate versus 3.7% among entrants in an external competition, and its best design outperformed the winner of 245 submissions.
  • Anthropic cautions that minibinders are not a standard therapeutic format and that a high-affinity binder is only the first step toward a drug-like molecule.
